# Many Stories — Candidate Screenplay v0.1

## Production status

Candidate narration and image order for the complete 24-still storyboard pass. Nothing in this document is Approved canon yet.

Target duration: approximately 3:30. Narration target: 108–115 words per minute, with space for natural sound and pauses.

## Movement I — Inheritance

### Shot 01 — The room remembers — 9 seconds

The Weaver works alone at the domestic loom in soft winter daylight.

### Shot 02 — Tension — 7 seconds

Her hands guide the shuttle and keep the thread under tension.

### Shot 03 — At the threshold — 8 seconds

The Child appears quietly in the doorway and watches.

### Shot 04 — Listening — 9 seconds

The Weaver continues working and speaks without turning the moment into a lesson.

### Shot 05 — The loose end — 8 seconds

The Weaver places the Child’s hand beside a loose thread. The Child does not pull yet.

**Narration**

Before a story is called heritage, it is simply life. A hand remembers the tension of thread. A voice remembers the pause between two words. A child learns when to listen, when to pull, and when to leave a small imperfection in the cloth. No archive begins with a label. It begins with one person trusting another with what cannot be carried alone.

## Movement II — Fragility

### Shot 06 — The tide line — 8 seconds

High water reaches the entrance of a small coastal community archive.

### Shot 07 — Water inside — 8 seconds

An elder and a young adult lift archival boxes as seawater crosses the stone floor.

### Shot 08 — Above the water — 8 seconds

Hands place recordings, photographs and folded cloth on the highest temporary shelf.

### Shot 09 — Sand at the door — 8 seconds

Two residents clear windblown sand from the carved threshold of an earth-built home.

### Shot 10 — The last fluent voice — 9 seconds

An elderly speaker records words in a quiet kitchen while a younger relative listens closely.

**Narration**

Some inheritances disappear in a single night. Water enters a room. Fire crosses a valley. A family is forced to leave. Others retreat almost silently: one speaker, one forgotten name, one gesture no one asks about until the hands that knew it are gone. A monument can be moved. A living relationship is harder to carry. Protection must begin before memory becomes evidence of an absence.

## Movement III — Return

### Shot 11 — Behind glass — 8 seconds

A handwoven textile fragment sits alone in a sterile metropolitan museum case.

### Shot 12 — The empty place — 8 seconds

A community guardian touches the faded outline where the textile once hung.

### Shot 13 — Packing the return — 8 seconds

A conservator carefully places the textile in a transport crate. No label is visible.

### Shot 14 — Arrival — 8 seconds

Community members receive the closed crate at a modest local cultural house.

### Shot 15 — Opening — 10 seconds

The crate opens on a worktable. The people around it remain quiet and attentive.

**Narration**

For generations, protection often meant removal. A song became a recording owned by a stranger. A textile survived behind glass while the room that gave it meaning held an empty space. The object remained. The relationship around it did not. Returning what was taken cannot repair time, and it does not erase the people who benefited from keeping it. But return restores something essential: the right to decide what happens next.

## Movement IV — Care

### Shot 16 — Learning again — 9 seconds

Young weavers study the returned textile beside an elder and begin a new piece.

### Shot 17 — The community records itself — 9 seconds

A local archivist records an elder’s voice while another community member logs the session on an unreadable laptop.

### Shot 18 — A room prepared for water — 8 seconds

The coastal archive now has permanently raised shelves and a floodable stone floor.

### Shot 19 — Moving the threshold — 9 seconds

Residents and conservation workers relocate a carved wooden doorway away from a collapsing shoreline.

### Shot 20 — The trade — 7 seconds

Affluent bidders sit beneath a spotlit antiquity in a restrained international auction room.

### Shot 21 — The repair — 9 seconds

The community conservation workshop receives timber, archival materials and practical tools.

**Narration**

Care is not the attempt to freeze life at the moment outsiders first recorded it. A language may enter a microphone and still belong to its speakers. A building may move inland and remain part of a place. A pattern may change in the hands of the next generation without becoming ownerless. Those who profit from the trade in the past can help pay for repair, but funding does not purchase authority. The community that carries the knowledge must remain sovereign over how it is kept, translated, shared, refused, or renewed.

## Movement V — Plurality

### Shot 22 — A living voice — 9 seconds

A young musician works with the locally controlled language recording while the elder listens in the same modest studio.

### Shot 23 — The house is alive — 10 seconds

The adapted coastal cultural house is active again: children, elders and working-age adults use it in ordinary daily life.

### Shot 24 — The next hands — 12 seconds

Back in the Weaver’s room, the Child operates the shuttle. The Weaver watches nearby. The cloth is unfinished.

**Narration**

The world once imagined that many stories would pull it apart. So it collected them, classified them, and tried to speak for them. But harmony is not sameness. It is the difficult practice of remaining together without demanding that every voice become one voice. A protected story is not sealed. It can breathe, travel, answer, and change—without forgetting who has the right to carry it.

The world does not gain a single story. It gains a way to protect its many stories.

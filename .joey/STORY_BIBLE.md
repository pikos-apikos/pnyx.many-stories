# Many Stories — Minimal Story Bible

## Status

Version 0.1. Approved inputs are limited to the festival track and the temporary visual DNA. The screenplay and every generated still remain Candidates until Yiannis approves them.

## Destination

A 3:20–3:50 narrator-led short film for the Higgsfield Global Film Festival. The film transforms the PNyx “Global Treaty for Cultural Heritage” into a human-scale cinematic allegory. It must feel like an observed story, not a policy explainer or product demonstration.

## Premise

Culture survives when one person entrusts part of a living practice to another. Culture becomes fragile when climate, displacement, extraction or silence breaks that relationship. Protection is not possession. Protection is the restoration of a community’s ability to carry its own story forward.

## Thesis

Humanity does not need one shared story. Humanity needs a shared way to protect many living stories without taking ownership away from the people who carry them.

## Emotional question

What does it mean to protect something without taking it away from the life that gives it meaning?

## Reality level and genre

- Photoreal observational documentary.
- Fictional people who do not resemble identifiable real people.
- Plausible present-day locations and actions.
- No supernatural events and no magical visual metaphor.
- The recurring thread is physical thread, not a glowing or impossible symbol.

## Structure

1. **Inheritance** — culture begins as ordinary life passed between people.
2. **Fragility** — water, sand, silence and extraction interrupt transmission.
3. **Return** — survival behind glass is not the same as belonging; return restores agency.
4. **Care** — communities record, adapt, relocate, repair and decide.
5. **Plurality** — living culture changes without becoming ownerless or uniform.

## Temporary visual DNA

**Selected reference: Weaver Candidate A.**

Use the reference for:

- raw observational realism;
- soft natural window light;
- low saturation and restrained contrast;
- cool gray ambient light with warm worn wood and fiber;
- tactile hands, fabric, dust, stone, paper and imperfect rooms;
- candid gestures and quiet dignity;
- a camera that witnesses rather than directs.

Do not use the reference to make all people, homes or communities look alike. Do not copy the woman’s identity into unrelated scenes.

## Frame and camera grammar

- 16:9 landscape frame for every storyboard still.
- Predominantly eye-level 35 mm or 50 mm documentary framing.
- Use medium-wide frames for relationships and restrained close frames for hands and objects.
- Keep the camera physically plausible and mostly static.
- Leave breathing space in the frame. Do not center every subject.
- Prefer available daylight and practical interior light.
- Preserve shadow detail. Avoid cinematic teal-and-orange grading.
- Add subtle natural film grain. Avoid artificial sharpness and glossy skin.

## Color and material palette

- Weathered timber, undyed wool, faded cloth, limestone, earth plaster, paper, oxidized metal and seawater.
- Charcoal, brown, gray, muted cream, faded rust and restrained blue-green.
- Later scenes may gain slightly warmer skin tones and modest color, but the film must not become glossy or celebratory advertising.

## Recurring motifs

- **Hands:** knowledge as practiced memory.
- **Thresholds:** the point where a story can leave, return or disappear.
- **Raised objects:** practical climate adaptation without spectacle.
- **Empty spaces:** the relationship missing around an extracted object.
- **Loose ends:** continuity without perfection or final closure.

## Recurring people

### The Weaver

The elderly woman from Weaver Candidate A appears in Shots 01–05 and Shot 24. Preserve her face, age, knit cap, patterned fleece, heavy working hands, domestic loom and lived-in room when the reference is used as an identity lock.

### The Child

A fictional child aged approximately 10–12 appears in Shots 03–05 and Shot 24. The child wears ordinary contemporary clothes in muted colors. The child observes before touching the loom. The child is not a sentimental symbol and does not perform for the camera.

### Global ensemble

All other people are fictional and community-specific. They appear as capable participants, not anonymous victims. Each location keeps its own architecture, clothing logic and material culture. Avoid generalized “traditional,” “tribal” or ceremonial styling.

## Cultural representation rules

- Do not recreate sacred ceremonies.
- Do not combine recognizable motifs from unrelated cultures into one costume, object or room.
- Do not present a community as frozen in the past.
- Show contemporary tools when they support continuity: microphones, laptops, archival boxes, raised shelving and transport equipment.
- Keep screens turned away or unreadable.
- Do not generate legible text, invented scripts, flags, logos or labels.
- Show consent, attention and local agency when recording language or knowledge.
- Repatriated material must return to community guardians, not to an abstract state authority.

## Prohibited visual drift

- polished travel advertising;
- luxury fashion editorial;
- fantasy glow, floating thread or mystical particles;
- sentimental posing or staged smiles;
- generic poverty imagery;
- museum spectacle or triumphal return ceremony;
- pristine sets without ordinary wear;
- crowds that hide the human action;
- duplicated people, malformed hands or impossible loom mechanics;
- visible brand marks, watermarks, captions or generated writing.

## Audio direction for the later stage

- One calm AI-generated narrator.
- Measured pace: approximately 108–115 words per minute.
- Natural room tone, loom movement, water, wind, crate wood, paper and distant voices.
- No identifiable real voice.
- Music, if used, must remain sparse and must not imitate a specific community’s sacred or ceremonial music.

## Ending

Return to the Weaver’s room. The child operates the shuttle while the Weaver watches without correcting every movement. The cloth remains unfinished. End on:

> The world does not gain a single story. It gains a way to protect its many stories.
